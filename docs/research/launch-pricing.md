# True Count launch subscription pricing

Research date: 2026-09-05. Decision ticket: [Launch subscription pricing evidence](https://github.com/0xRowdy/true-count/issues/9).

## Recommendation for discussion

Trial one premium tier at **US$5.99/month or US$39.99/year**, with the accepted premium benefits identical across billing periods. Annual billing works out to approximately $3.33/month and is 44.4% less than twelve monthly payments ($71.88). This is a provisional product judgment, not a measured optimum or an approved price. US/USD is the comparison assumption; launch countries and localized prices remain undecided.

Start without a separate introductory subscription trial. The accepted recurring free training allowance already lets users experience coaching before subscribing. Test a seven-day premium trial later if users cannot evaluate the premium analytics or offline benefits adequately. This trial recommendation remains unapproved and is not a claim that competitors prove trials are unnecessary.

## Interpretation, not market proof

The evidence spans inexpensive one-time tools, low-priced recreational subscriptions, and more expensive specialist training. It does not support calling a single number the market average. True Count's proposed subscription must earn its recurring price through unlimited coaching, counting challenges, useful comparisons and trends, all three games offline, community participation, and no ads. Ordinary free play, records, and private strategy creation already remain free under the accepted plan.

The proposed $5.99 monthly price gives recreational users a modest short commitment while staying below the displayed specialist poker Standard price. The $39.99 annual price is below the observed $49.99 roulette and $59.99 blackjack annual products, but above Aw Craps!'s $17.99. The three-game bundle and broader training tools are the reason to test that position; they do not prove that a person who plays only one game will value the bundle. Cheap BJA tools and free training are the strongest counterargument to charging much more at launch.

A low launch price is not a substitute for checking operating costs. No customer acquisition, conversion, renewal, hosting, simulation, moderation, or support-cost evidence was available. Competitor list prices reveal neither revenue nor willingness to pay. No purchases were made, no checkout was opened, and most evidence is iOS; Android subscription parity and localized taxes/prices need separate verification before release.

## Validation proposal

1. Before store setup, verify the selected products and billing periods in both platform consoles and review the final localized purchase screens. Show annual total billing prominently; the monthly equivalent is explanatory only. Recheck comparator listings near launch because this is a dated snapshot.
2. Start with $5.99/$39.99 and one free-allowance policy. Instrument account-level allowance exposure, allowance exhaustion, paywall exposure, plan choice, completed purchase, refund, cancellation, retained training use, and renewal. Report by launch platform and principal game.
3. Evaluate retained net revenue per eligible user alongside completed training and retention. Include platform deductions, refunds, and service costs; do not optimize purchase conversion alone. Follow monthly cohorts through at least two renewal opportunities. Early annual purchases do not establish annual renewal or lifetime value.
4. Once baseline traffic supports a useful comparison, test one price dimension at a time: for example $4.99 versus $5.99 monthly while holding annual at $39.99, then $39.99 versus $49.99 annual with a fixed monthly price. Predefine the minimum useful effect and sample requirements from the observed baseline; low traffic calls for qualitative interviews and longer observation, not claims of statistical certainty.
5. Keep pricing, free-allowance, and introductory-trial experiments separate initially. Otherwise a purchase change cannot be attributed to a price. Preserve assigned policy during activities as already agreed. Experiment prices apply to new eligible offers; do not silently change existing subscriber billing.
6. If premium value is hard to assess from free access, compare no introductory trial against a clearly disclosed seven-day trial while holding price and allowances fixed. Measure subsequent paid retention and refunds, not trial starts. Because publishing and ratings are gated to reduce spam, trial access to those actions needs an explicit decision before this experiment.

Pending user decisions: provisional launch prices, launch currency/markets, and whether to omit an introductory subscription trial. This report settles the evidence-gathering task, not those product choices.
