## Question

What monthly and annual launch subscription prices are defensible for True Count, given current first-party pricing for comparable table-game training apps and the accepted free/premium boundaries? Research direct competitors and relevant adjacent products, distinguish subscriptions from one-time purchases, note currency and storefront, and propose a starting price and validation approach. Consider introductory trials but do not make the user's pricing or trial decision.

## Answer

Completed first-party pricing research on 2026-09-05: [Launch subscription pricing report](../research/launch-pricing.md). The evidence includes inexpensive one-time trainers and subscriptions across recreational and specialist products, with currency, storefront, and ambiguous IAP labels distinguished.

The report proposes US$5.99/month or US$39.99/year and no separate introductory subscription trial initially, with a validation plan. These are hypotheses for user review, not approved prices or trial policy. Competitor pricing does not establish willingness to pay or operating margins. US/USD remains a comparison assumption; Android subscription offers and localized pricing require verification before release.
